<section class="dg-card">
  <h2 class="dg-label">Message Recap</h2>
  <div class="dg-prose">
    <p>Too many of us walk through life carrying a heavy doubt about whether God actually loves us as individuals. We may nod along to the idea that God loves the world, but deep down we feel like our personal history, habits, and failures disqualify us.</p>
    <p>This is not just a theological issue—it shapes how we pray, how we love others, and how we live. Performance-driven thinking teaches us to measure our worth by our output. It leads us to shame, guilt, and a strange judgmentalism toward others. The gospel says the opposite: God's love is fixed, objective, and unearned.</p>
    <p>We don't believe in order to make God love us; we believe because He already does. When that truth lands in the heart, the shame and striving begin to melt. We can finally stop earning what has already been given.</p>
  </div>
</section>

<aside class="dg-big-idea">
  <div class="dg-big-idea-label">Main Idea</div>
  <blockquote>You cannot be a fully devoted follower of Jesus until you come to terms with the Father's love for you.</blockquote>
</aside>

    <section class="dg-devo-panel active" id="devo-monday">
      <a class="dg-devo-scripture-pill" href="https://www.bible.com/bible/111/PHP.3.4.NIV" target="_blank" rel="noopener">📖 Philippians 3:4–7</a>
      <div class="dg-prose">
        <p>Our minds are wired for scorekeeping. We assume acceptance must be earned through performance. That is a devastating way to approach God. It turns faith into a constant effort to prove we are good enough.</p>
        <p>Paul knew this trap intimately. By every external standard, he was a religious success. But when he encountered Jesus, he realized that achievement cannot buy affection. In the kingdom, love is not a reward for effort; it is a gift from the Father.</p>
        <p><strong>Reflect:</strong></p>
        <ul>
          <li>What spiritual achievements are you leaning on to convince yourself you are acceptable to God?</li>
          <li>When your mind says, "If God really knew me, He would be disappointed," how can you respond with the truth of grace?</li>
        </ul>
      </div>
    </section>

    <section class="dg-devo-panel" id="devo-tuesday">
      <a class="dg-devo-scripture-pill" href="https://www.bible.com/bible/111/JHN.13.34.NIV" target="_blank" rel="noopener">📖 John 13:34</a>
      <div class="dg-prose">
        <p>We often know God is love in our heads but not in our hearts. That creates a vague guilt that follows us into prayer, worship, and relationships. We feel like outsiders even when we are deeply loved.</p>
        <p>Jesus invites us to bring those feelings honestly before Him. We don't hide our shame from a loving Father. We invite Him into it. Grace is not a polite sentiment—it is a powerful, unrelenting affection that meets us in the middle of our failure.</p>
        <p><strong>Reflect:</strong></p>
        <ul>
          <li>What does the "shadow of shame" look like in your life?</li>
          <li>Where can you receive the reality of God's love without trying to fix yourself first?</li>
        </ul>
      </div>
    </section>

    <section class="dg-devo-panel" id="devo-wednesday">
      <a class="dg-devo-scripture-pill" href="https://www.bible.com/bible/111/PHP.3.6.NIV" target="_blank" rel="noopener">📖 Philippians 3:6, 9</a>
      <div class="dg-prose">
        <p>There are scenes in our lives where we are tempted to prove ourselves. We hide, defend, justify, or compensate. But the good news is this: God does not call us to earn a new status. He calls us to see the status we already have in Christ.</p>
        <p>It is one thing to know the gospel in theory. It is another to trust it emotionally. When the truth of God's love settles in the heart, the urgency to prove ourselves loosens.</p>
        <p><strong>Reflect:</strong></p>
        <ul>
          <li>What are you trying to prove right now?</li>
          <li>How would your relationships change if you trusted that you are already fully loved by God?</li>
        </ul>
      </div>
    </section>

    <section class="dg-devo-panel" id="devo-thursday">
      <a class="dg-devo-scripture-pill" href="https://www.bible.com/bible/111/ROM.8.1.NIV" target="_blank" rel="noopener">📖 Romans 8:1</a>
      <div class="dg-prose">
        <p>Condemnation is a powerful voice, but it is not final. It tells us that if God really knew the truth, He would reject us. But the gospel says there is no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus.</p>
        <p>To live in grace is to refuse the lie that love must be earned. It is to rest in a Father whose affection is not conditioned on your moral performance.</p>
        <p><strong>Reflect:</strong></p>
        <ul>
          <li>Where do you most often hear the voice of condemnation?</li>
          <li>What would it look like to answer that voice with the truth of Romans 8?</li>
        </ul>
      </div>
    </section>

    <section class="dg-devo-panel" id="devo-friday">
      <a class="dg-devo-scripture-pill" href="https://www.bible.com/bible/111/1JN.4.19.NIV" target="_blank" rel="noopener">📖 1 John 4:19</a>
      <div class="dg-prose">
        <p>Grace is not merely for us to receive in private. It is meant to shape how we love the people around us. When we receive God's love, we become less harsh, more patient, and more gracious.</p>
        <p>We cannot love others deeply while secretly believing we are unlovable. The Father calls us into the same kind of love He has shown us.</p>
        <p><strong>Reflect:</strong></p>
        <ul>
          <li>Who in your life needs the same patience and mercy you have received?</li>
          <li>How can you show grace to someone this week without keeping score?</li>
        </ul>
      </div>
    </section>
