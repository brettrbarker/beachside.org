<section class="dg-card">
  <h2 class="dg-label">Message Recap</h2>
  <div class="dg-prose">
    <p>The heart of discipleship does not rest on our ability to white-knuckle our way into spiritual perfection. It rests on our willingness to surrender to one unshakable reality: God loves us, and we have no say in the matter.</p>
    <p>For many of us, church culture has taught us to live as though God's affection is measured by performance. We assume everyone else is more disciplined, more mature, and more spiritually together than we are. That fear leaves us standing on the edge of the room, hoping no one notices how far short we feel. The gospel turns that story upside down. It reminds us that we do not become acceptable to God by behaving better. We are already loved by a Father who is not waiting for us to earn His affection.</p>
    <p>Paul's letter to the Galatians exposes the trap of law-keeping. Rules can reveal failure, but they cannot create transformation. Only grace can do that. The Christian life is not a series of self-imposed checks to earn favor. It is the life of Christ lived through us by the Spirit.</p>
  </div>
</section>

<aside class="dg-big-idea">
  <div class="dg-big-idea-label">Main Idea</div>
  <blockquote>It is a reorientation toward God: from "I promise" to "I surrender," from obedience to dependence, from "I will" to "I can't, but You can through me."</blockquote>
</aside>

    <section class="dg-devo-panel active" id="devo-monday">
      <a class="dg-devo-scripture-pill" href="https://www.bible.com/bible/111/GAL.5.4.NIV" target="_blank" rel="noopener">📖 Galatians 5:4–5</a>
      <div class="dg-prose">
        <p>We carry an invisible ledger around in our minds. We assume that love, worth, and approval have to be earned. So we bring that same performance model into our relationship with God. We tell ourselves that if we were more disciplined, we would feel closer to Him.</p>
        <p>Paul confronts that lie. When we try to establish our standing before God through rule-keeping, we are not drawing near to grace—we are resisting it. God's love is not earned, diminished by weakness, or thwarted by failure. It is a gift given by grace.</p>
        <p><strong>Reflect:</strong></p>
        <ul>
          <li>What specific performance-based lie does your mind repeat about why God must be disappointed in you?</li>
          <li>How would your anxiety shift if you truly believed God's love for you was beyond your control and not dependent on your performance?</li>
        </ul>
      </div>
    </section>

    <section class="dg-devo-panel" id="devo-tuesday">
      <a class="dg-devo-scripture-pill" href="https://www.bible.com/bible/111/GAL.5.1.NIV" target="_blank" rel="noopener">📖 Galatians 5:1</a>
      <div class="dg-prose">
        <p>Freedom is not a vague idea—it is the emotional and spiritual reality of living without the exhausting weight of self-justification. When we live by rules alone, we carry guilt, fear, and shame. We can feel spiritually good for a moment and collapse the next time we fail.</p>
        <p>Jesus doesn't call us into a better system of self-discipline. He calls us into freedom. The Father is not a law-enforcement officer waiting to issue a citation. He is a loving Father who runs toward us in our brokenness.</p>
        <p><strong>Reflect:</strong></p>
        <ul>
          <li>When you feel spiritually failed, do you hide from God or run to Him?</li>
          <li>What would emotional freedom look like in your life this week?</li>
        </ul>
      </div>
    </section>

    <section class="dg-devo-panel" id="devo-wednesday">
      <a class="dg-devo-scripture-pill" href="https://www.bible.com/bible/111/GAL.2.20.NIV" target="_blank" rel="noopener">📖 Galatians 2:20</a>
      <div class="dg-prose">
        <p>Our willpower is real, but its limits are real too. We make promises to God, fail, feel guilty, and make the same promises again. That cycle is exhausting. Rules never empower us to keep rules.</p>
        <p>Jesus invites us from "I promise" to "I surrender." The shift is not passivity; it is the courage to stop trying to live by our own strength and allow Christ to live through us.</p>
        <p><strong>Reflect:</strong></p>
        <ul>
          <li>In what area of life are you exhausted from trying to outwork your weakness?</li>
          <li>What would it look like to lay down your will today and invite Christ to live His life through you?</li>
        </ul>
      </div>
    </section>

    <section class="dg-devo-panel" id="devo-thursday">
      <a class="dg-devo-scripture-pill" href="https://www.bible.com/bible/111/GAL.5.16.NIV" target="_blank" rel="noopener">📖 Galatians 5:16–17</a>
      <div class="dg-prose">
        <p>Our spiritual formation is not separate from our bodies. When we ignore sleep, rest, movement, and healthy rhythms, we become more reactive, more frazzled, and less sensitive to the Spirit. We cannot overlook the body if we want to walk in step with God.</p>
        <p>Walking by the Spirit means caring for the body God gave you so your mind and heart can listen well. Rest is not a moral failure; it is a spiritual practice.</p>
        <p><strong>Reflect:</strong></p>
        <ul>
          <li>What current patterns of exhaustion or overwork are making you more vulnerable to agitation and unhealthy appetite?</li>
          <li>What action can you take today to create a more spiritual, restful rhythm?</li>
        </ul>
      </div>
    </section>

    <section class="dg-devo-panel" id="devo-friday">
      <a class="dg-devo-scripture-pill" href="https://www.bible.com/bible/111/GAL.5.13.NIV" target="_blank" rel="noopener">📖 Galatians 5:13–14</a>
      <div class="dg-prose">
        <p>The goal of grace is never isolated self-improvement. Grace forms us so we can love people well. The only metric that matters in the kingdom is faith expressing itself through love.</p>
        <p>Freedom from the law is not freedom to indulge ourselves; it is freedom to serve others the way Christ served us. When our hearts are grounded in God's love, our relationships become less about getting and more about giving.</p>
        <p><strong>Reflect:</strong></p>
        <ul>
          <li>Where has your love been shaped more by rights and expectations than by grace?</li>
          <li>How can you love one person well this week in a way that reflects Christ's love to you?</li>
        </ul>
      </div>
    </section>
